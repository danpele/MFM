What Is a Bubble?

A Simulated Rational Bubble

    Question for the room The bubble survives each period with probability 0.97 and the required return is 1%. How fast does it grow while it survives, and how long does it last on average?
    Answer

    It grows by (1 + r)/π − 1 ≈ 4.1% per period, so that its expected growth stays at r = 1%. Its expected life is 1/(1 − π) ≈ 33 periods. In the simulation the bubble collapsed visibly 4 times, and at its peak the price was 1.43 times the fundamental value.

    Run-Ups and Crashes in the Data

    Run-Ups and Crashes Aligned at the Peak

      Question for the room The run-ups over the two years before the peak differ by orders of magnitude. Do the falls after the peak differ as much?
      Answer

      No. The run-ups range from +97% (BET, 2007) to +124,004% (Ethereum, 2018), while the maximum drawdowns over the four years after the peak lie between −74% (Tesla) and −94% (Ethereum). The episodes were chosen because they ended in a crash, so they do not show how often a run-up is followed by one.

      What Do Sharp Run-Ups Predict?

        Question for the room After an industry's two-year return exceeds 100%, how often does a 40% crash follow?
        Answer

        In 45% of the cases (53 episodes in 27 industries, 95% CI [27%, 62%]), against 11% for an arbitrary industry-month. The mean return over the next two years is only 2%, with CI [−13%, 20%]: a sharp run-up raises the risk of a crash, but does not make an average loss predictable.

        Testing for Explosive Behaviour

        Three Ways to Choose Windows

          Question for the room With T = 440 months, how many ADF regressions does GSADF run with the minimum window of the PSY rule?
          Answer

          The rule gives r₀ = 0.01 + 1.8/√440 = 0.096, i.e. w₀ = 42 months. SADF runs 399 windows (one per end point), GSADF 79,800 (all start–end pairs), hence the supremum over about 80,000 windows.

          Null Distributions and Critical Values

            Question for the room Why is the 95% critical value of GSADF (2.19) so much larger than that of a single ADF (−0.06)?
            Answer

            GSADF is the maximum of about 80,000 ADF statistics on overlapping windows, and under the null the maximum of many statistics typically exceeds each of them. With a shorter minimum window there are more windows and the critical value rises (2.58 for w₀ = 22); for w₀ = 132 it falls to 1.65. The full-sample ADF does not depend on w₀.

            S&P Composite (Shiller) Price-Dividend Ratio, 1871–2026

              Question for the room The price-dividend ratio reached its sample maximum, 91.2, in May 2026. Why is it not date-stamped as an explosive episode?
              Answer

              The test looks for acceleration, not for a high level. BSADF exceeds its critical value only in Aug 1917–Jun 1918 (an accelerating fall of the ratio), Nov 1928–Sep 1929 and May 1997–Aug 2001. GSADF = 3.41 against a 95% critical value of 2.39, and the full-sample ADF (−1.11) detects nothing.

              Bitcoin: Explosive Episodes

                Question for the room Bitcoin rose to new highs in 2024–2025. Is this rise an explosive episode as well?
                Answer

                No. With the Monte Carlo critical values the episodes are Apr 2017–Jun 2018 and Dec 2020–May 2021; with the wild bootstrap, May 2017–Jan 2018 and Dec 2020–Feb 2021. A steady rise is not an explosive acceleration. GSADF = 3.85 rejects the null with both critical values (2.27 and 2.82).

                Date-Stamping Is a Multiple Test: Bitcoin

                  Question for the room Date-stamping compares BSADF with its critical value in each of the 626 weeks. How likely is at least one false alarm?
                  Answer

                  On 2,000 random-walk paths, an exceedance occurs in 95% of them and an "episode" in 59%. The family-wise critical value over 104 weeks is 1.75, against pointwise Monte Carlo values of at most 0.71; with it a single episode remains, Oct 2017–Jan 2018.

                  The Log-Periodic Power Law (LPPLS)

                  LPPLS on Bitcoin, 2017

                    Question for the room The LPPLS fit up to 15 November 2017 puts tc on 21 Dec 2017, 5 days after the peak. Is it a qualified fit?
                    Answer

                    No. m = 0.95, ω = 10.54 and B = −2.62 lie in the required ranges, but the maximum relative error of the fitted price is 0.42, above the 0.15 limit. No fit on this grid is qualified, and the estimated tc moves by weeks as t₂ advances.

                    How Stable Is the Critical Time?

                      Question for the room If LPPLS anticipated the crash, the estimated tc should stay close to the peak as t₂ advances. Does it?
                      Answer

                      No. For Bitcoin, the 20 fits before the peak put tc between Oct 2017 and Feb 2018, with a mean absolute error of 48 days; only 20% are within 30 days of the peak. For the Nasdaq 100 the mean error is 70 days, and tc often follows the date of the estimate. None of these fits is qualified.

                      Bitcoin: LPPLS Confidence Indicator

                        Question for the room After an LPPLS signal (positive indicator), is a 20% fall within 90 days more frequent than without one?
                        Answer

                        No. The fall follows 22% of the 82 signals and 37% of the dates without a signal (709 dates evaluated). The difference, −15.5 percentage points, has a block-bootstrap CI of [−30.0, 1.8]: the evidence is weak, and the sign is the opposite of the expected one.

                        LPPLS: Qualification Filters

                          Question for the room Which condition removes the most LPPLS fits on Bitcoin?
                          Answer

                          Taken separately, the maximum relative error of at most 0.15: only 10% of the windows pass it, against about a third for the conditions on m, ω, tc and the number of oscillations. After the Lomb test, 0.3% of the windows remain qualified for Bitcoin and 3.5% for the Nasdaq 100, hence the small values of the indicator (at most 0.18).

                          Regime-Switching Models

                          Nasdaq 100: Two Regimes

                            Question for the room The turbulent regime of the Nasdaq 100 has a volatility of 5.40% a week. How long does it last on average, and what does the filtered probability show at the March 2000 peak?
                            Answer

                            With p₂₂ = 0.957 the expected duration is 23.5 weeks (calm regime: 75.4). The filtered probability is 0.99 at the end of March 2000, while prices were still rising: the model signals high volatility, not direction. By the smoothed probability, the turbulent episode around the peak starts in Jun 1998.

                            How Many Regimes? A Bootstrap LR Test

                              Question for the room Why do we not use the χ² distribution for the LR test of one against two regimes?
                              Answer

                              Under H₀ the transition probabilities p₁₁ and p₂₂ are not identified, so LR does not follow a χ² distribution. The 95% bootstrap critical value is 11.8 for Bitcoin, against 9.5 from χ²₄. The observed LR, 123.2, exceeds all 199 replications, so p = 1/(B + 1) = 0.005, the smallest p-value possible with B = 199.

                              Crypto and AI Bubbles

                              Dot-Com vs AI

                                Question for the room Forty months after the first explosive signal, is the AI-era rise of the Nasdaq 100 comparable to that of the 1990s?
                                Answer

                                No. In the AI episode (first signal in May 2023) the level is 2.1 times that at the signal after 40 months; in the 1990s (first signal in Jun 1995) it was 2.6 times, and the peak came after 57 months, at 8.2 times. NVIDIA stands at 9.6 times after 43 months; Cisco peaked after 16 months, at 4.1 times.

                                Case Study: Speculators and Volume in a Housing Boom

                                Case Study (3/5): Prices and Volume, 2000–2011

                                  Question for the room Which peaks first in the US housing boom: volume or prices?
                                  Answer

                                  Volume. New-home sales, adjusted for the calendar month, peak at 157 in Jul 2005, while prices rise to 176 in Jul 2006: a 12-month quiet phase, with sales already down to 110 at the price peak. With unadjusted sales the quiet phase lasts 16 months. In 2011 the averages are 35 for sales and 133 for prices.
