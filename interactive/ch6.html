When Do Markets Move Together?

Stocks and Bonds: A Correlation That Changed Sign

    Question for the room Until 2021 the 252-day SPY–TLT correlation was almost always negative. What changed from 2022?
    Answer

    The correlation turned positive: it was positive on 6% of the days before 2022 and on 81% of the days from January 2022. Growth shocks move stocks and bonds in opposite directions; inflation shocks, which dominated in 2022, move them in the same direction.

    Measuring Covariance and Its Uncertainty

    Daily, 2-Day and Weekly Correlations, 2000–2026

      Question for the room The daily SPY–Euro Stoxx 50 correlation is 0.59. Why does it rise to 0.79 on weekly returns?
      Answer

      Euro-area exchanges close before New York, so US news released after the European close enters European prices only the next day. The same-day correlation is biased towards zero, and the lagged correlation (SPY yesterday, Euro Stoxx 50 today) is 0.20. Over longer horizons the one-day lag matters less and less.

      Fisher versus HAC Intervals: SPY and TLT

        Question for the room Is the 95% Fisher interval adequate for the correlation of two daily return series?
        Answer

        No. The Fisher formula assumes i.i.d. pairs from the bivariate Normal distribution. With fat tails and volatility clustering the HAC interval is wider (the median ratio of the widths is 1.24), and the conclusion of the test changes in 8.2% of the windows. Most of the difference comes from the fourth moments, not from autocorrelation.

        EWMA versus Rolling Windows, SPY–TLT since 2019

          Question for the room Which estimator was the first to signal the positive correlation of 2022?
          Answer

          EWMA with λ = 0.94: the signal of 20 consecutive days of positive correlation comes on 10 May 2022, and for the 250-day window only on 8 December 2022. Speed has a price: the standard deviation of the daily change of the correlation is 0.060 for EWMA with λ = 0.94 and 0.0088 for the 250-day window.

          Multivariate GARCH and DCC

          DCC Correlation of SPY and TLT, 2002–2026

            Question for the room The DCC estimates are a = 0.052 and b = 0.934. What does their sum tell us?
            Answer

            It is the persistence of the correlation: a + b = 0.986, so a shock to Q_t halves in about 48 days. The LR test against constant correlation gives 533.4; the bootstrap under CCC gives p = 0.005, the smallest possible value with B = 199.

            Application: Minimum-Variance Weights with DCC

              Question for the room By how much does DCC reduce the volatility of the minimum-variance portfolio compared with a constant correlation?
              Answer

              By very little: 9.06% with DCC, against 9.10% with a constant correlation and 10.99% for the fixed 60/40 portfolio. Most of the gain comes from the GARCH volatilities. The parameters are estimated on the full sample, so the comparison is in-sample.

              Crisis Correlations and the Forbes–Rigobon Bias

              How Much Can Volatility Alone Raise a Correlation?

                Question for the room In the 2008 crisis the variance of 2-day S&P 500 returns rose 7.5 times. How would an unchanged true correlation of 0.4 have been measured?
                Answer

                As 0.77. With δ = 6.54, the formula ρ_c = ρ√((1 + δ)/(1 + δρ²)) raises the measured correlation although the transmission coefficient β stays the same. A true correlation of 0.2 would have been measured as 0.49.

                2008: Raw versus Adjusted Crisis Correlations

                  Question for the room The S&P 500–Euro Stoxx 50 correlation rose from 0.61 in the calm year to 0.80 in the 2008 crisis. Is this contagion?
                  Answer

                  Not under the assumptions of the model. The raw rise is significant (p = 0.005), but the correlation adjusted for volatility is 0.44, below its calm-year level (p = 0.93). For the BET, 0.48 becomes 0.20 (p = 0.55). The numbers change when the windows are shifted, so their choice has to be justified.

                  Copulas and Tail Dependence

                  Exceedance Correlations: S&P 500 and Euro Stoxx 50

                    Question for the room At q = 0.10, how correlated are the joint losses of the S&P 500 and the Euro Stoxx 50, compared with the Normal distribution?
                    Answer

                    The correlation of joint losses is 0.80 (86 weeks) and that of joint gains 0.69 (70 weeks). The bivariate Normal distribution with the same correlation, 0.78, gives only 0.41 at the same threshold.

                    Five Copulas with the Same Kendall's Tau

                      Question for the room The five copulas have the same Kendall's tau, 0.5. Do they give the same probability of joint crashes?
                      Answer

                      No. The Gaussian and Frank copulas have λ_L = 0. The t copula with ν = 4 has λ_L = λ_U = 0.40, Clayton has λ_L = 0.71, and Gumbel has dependence only in the upper tail, with λ_U = 0.59.

                      From Residuals to Pseudo-Observations: JPM and BAC

                        Question for the room Why do we estimate the copula on the ranks of the GARCH residuals rather than on the returns?
                        Answer

                        A copula assumes i.i.d. observations; the GARCH filter removes volatility clustering, which would otherwise mix with the dependence. Ranks divided by T + 1 give uniform margins without choosing a marginal distribution. Rank measures do not depend on the margins: for JPM and BAC, τ = 0.641 and ρ_S = 0.822.

                        Copula Estimates and Goodness of Fit: JPM and BAC

                          Question for the room The Student t copula has the lowest AIC. Does that make it an adequate model?
                          Answer

                          Not necessarily. AIC only ranks the families. The Rosenblatt test rejects all five, with the smallest possible p-value; the t copula has the smallest statistic, 0.082. As ν grows, the t copula approaches the Gaussian one and its AIC advantage of 309 points shrinks.

                          Empirical Tail Dependence: JPM and BAC

                            Question for the room When JPM has one of its worst 5% days, how often does BAC have one of its own worst 5% days?
                            Answer

                            In about 61% of the cases (λ_L(0.05) = 0.61), inside the band of the fitted t copula (0.57–0.65). The upper tail, 0.55, lies below the band: joint gains are weaker than the t copula, which is symmetric, predicts.

                            Applications: Europe, Romania and Banks

                            US, Euro-Area and Romanian Equities, 2000–2026

                              Question for the room Is the Romanian market more closely linked to the euro area now than in the 2000s?
                              Answer

                              On average, yes: the Euro Stoxx 50–BET correlation is 0.33 in 2000–2009, 0.45 in 2010–2019 and 0.61 since 2020. On the rolling window it jumps in crises (0.76 at the end of 2021) and falls back afterwards (0.34 in September 2026), so part of the rise may be the Forbes–Rigobon volatility bias.

                              Banks: Correlations of Weekly Returns, 2010–2026

                                Question for the room Which links between banks of different regions are the weakest?
                                Answer

                                The links with the Romanian banks: BNP–TLV 0.33 and JPM–BRD 0.30, against 0.86 for JPM–BAC and 0.75 for DBK–BNP. Country factors (sovereign risk, domestic interest rates, regulation) separate the banking systems.

                                Bank Correlations over Time

                                  Question for the room What happened to the link between the Romanian banks and the others in March–June 2020?
                                  Answer

                                  The average 52-week correlation rose to 0.56, against 0.25 over the whole period, i.e. more than twice as high. Diversification across banking systems works in normal times and weakens in global shocks.

                                  A Correlation Network of Banks

                                    Question for the room Through which edge are the Romanian banks connected to the rest of the network?
                                    Answer

                                    Through BNP–BRD, with a correlation of 0.35. The other edges are JPM–BAC (0.86), DBK–BNP (0.75), JPM–BNP (0.65) and TLV–BRD (0.65). The tree is undirected and contemporaneous, so it does not show the direction of transmission.

                                    AI for Scientific Discovery

                                    Mini-Case: Does Inflation Drive the Stock–Bond Correlation?

                                      Question for the room Does the level of inflation explain the positive SPY–TLT correlation?
                                      Answer

                                      On the full sample the slope is 0.055 per percentage point of inflation (t = 2.96), but on 2002–2021, when inflation varied without a change of regime, it is 0.002 (t = 0.06). The slope comes from the episode after 2022, and a single change of regime cannot separate the level of inflation from the monetary-policy regime.
