Mean–Variance Optimisation

Two Assets: How Correlation Diversifies

    Question for the room The annual volatilities are 14.7% for SPY and 13.5% for TLT. Can a mix of the two have a volatility below 10%?
    Answer

    Yes. With a correlation of −0.10, the minimum-variance portfolio holds 46% in SPY and has a volatility of 9.4%. Portfolio volatility equals the weighted average of the volatilities only when the correlation is 1.

    The Frontier of US Sectors

      Question for the room Does the tangency portfolio hold every sector with a positive mean return?
      Answer

      No. With short sales allowed, it is short XLB and XLF, although both have positive means. XLB has a correlation of 0.85 with XLI, and XLF has the lowest Sharpe ratio. The long-only constraint lowers the Sharpe ratio from 0.66 to 0.61.

      Estimation Risk

      Error Maximisation: Estimated versus Realised Frontiers

        Question for the room With 60 months of data, how often does the estimated tangency portfolio beat 1/N?
        Answer

        In about 4% of the samples. The median realised Sharpe ratio is 0.30, against 0.54 for 1/N, while the median in-sample estimate is 1.56.

        Unstable Weights

          Question for the room How large do mean–variance weights become when they are re-estimated each month on the last 60 months?
          Answer

          The median gross exposure (sum of absolute weights) is 4.9 for MV and 1.5 for GMV-LW. The largest absolute MV weight is 26; in 90% of the months the MV gross exposure exceeds 3.

          How Long Must the Window Be to Beat 1/N?

            Question for the room How many months of data does the estimated mean–variance portfolio need to beat 1/N?
            Answer

            In the simulation, the median MV Sharpe ratio exceeds the 1/N value (0.54) only at T = 480 months, i.e. 40 years. GMV and ERC pass 1/N much earlier because they do not use estimated means.

            Covariance Shrinkage

            Sample Eigenvalues versus the Noise Band

              Question for the room How many of the 16 eigenvalues of the correlation matrix lie above the Marchenko–Pastur band?
              Answer

              Only one, the common market factor, which explains 56% of the total variance. The other 15 lie in the band that pure noise with the same N/T ratio also produces.

              Shrinkage on US Sectors

                Question for the room What happens to the extreme eigenvalues as δ increases?
                Answer

                Large eigenvalues fall and small ones rise, so the condition number drops (from 52 to 32 at δ = 0.24, the Ledoit–Wolf value on the last 60 months). The GMV weights become less extreme.

                Black–Litterman

                Black–Litterman on US Sectors

                  Question for the room The view is that XLK beats XLU by 3% a year. How do the weights of the other seven sectors change?
                  Answer

                  They do not. The XLK weight rises and the XLU weight falls by the same amount, 3.3 percentage points. The means of the other sectors move through their correlations with XLK and XLU, but their weights stay at 1/N.

                  Risk Parity and Hierarchical Risk Parity

                  Weights versus Risk Contributions

                    Question for the room In the 1/N portfolio, equities and high-yield bonds hold half of the capital. What share of the risk do they generate?
                    Answer

                    69%. Equal risk contribution (ERC) moves capital towards Treasuries: IEF receives 25%, and each asset contributes 12.5% of the risk.

                    HRP on 16 ETFs

                      Question for the room Why does HRP not invert the covariance matrix?
                      Answer

                      HRP orders the assets by the correlation tree and splits the weights recursively by the variance of each group. It uses only group variances, so it avoids the inversion that amplifies estimation errors. Long-only GMV puts non-zero weights on only 5 of the 16 ETFs.

                      Out-of-Sample Evaluation

                      Out-of-Sample Growth: Multi-Asset

                        Question for the room Which rule has the highest out-of-sample Sharpe ratio in the multi-asset universe?
                        Answer

                        MV-LO, with 0.80, followed by 1/N with 0.52 and ERC with 0.49. Unconstrained MV has −0.22 and a mean monthly turnover of 44.

                        Sharpe Ratios Across Universes

                          Question for the room Does the ranking of the rules stay the same if the evaluation starts later?
                          Answer

                          No. The differences between 1/N, ERC, HRP and the long-only rules are small and change with the evaluation period; unconstrained MV stays last.

                          Sharpe Ratios after Costs

                            Question for the room At what cost per unit of turnover does unconstrained MV lose its Sharpe ratio in the combined universe?
                            Answer

                            The MV Sharpe ratio is 0.11 without costs and −0.51 at 50 bp, because its mean monthly turnover is 22. 1/N goes from 0.76 to 0.75.

                            Is Anything Better than 1/N?

                              Question for the room Is the difference between GMV-LW and 1/N on sectors significant?
                              Answer

                              No. The difference of Sharpe ratios is −0.02, with p = 0.93 (HAC) and p = 0.94 (bootstrap). None of the estimated rules beats 1/N significantly in the three universes.

                              A Portfolio of BVB Blue Chips

                              BVB Portfolios versus BET-TR

                                Question for the room Does any portfolio of BVB blue chips beat the BET-TR index?
                                Answer

                                MV-LO has a Sharpe ratio of 1.66 against 1.45 for BET-TR, but the difference is not significant (p = 0.44, HAC). 1/N has 1.41 and ERC 1.38.
