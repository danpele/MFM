A Short History of Markets and Financial Models

Five Centuries of Exchanges, Four Centuries of Crashes

    Question for the room VOC shares have traded since 1602; the chart starts in that year, and the ▶ button continues the timeline. How long did it take until the first bubble marked on the chart?
    Answer

    35 years: the tulip mania, in 1637. The Mississippi and South Sea bubbles only followed in 1720, 118 years after the VOC, and the Bucharest Stock Exchange opened in 1882.

    The 2000–2026 Landscape

    Risk and Return, 2015–2026

      Question for the room Over 2015–2026 the S&P 500 with dividends has a CAGR of 13.7% and gold one of 11.8%. Is the difference in mean returns statistically significant?
      Answer

      No. On common days, the S&P 500 minus gold gives +1.9 pp a year with an SE of 6.8 pp, so t = 0.28. Even for a single asset the standard error of the annualised mean return, σ/√Y with Y = 11.7 years, is 5.1 pp for the S&P 500 and 4.8 pp for gold. Daily data do not shrink this standard error: it depends on the number of years, not on the number of observations.

      Crises and Drawdowns

        Question for the room The S&P 500 fell by 57% from its peak of 9 October 2007 to 9 March 2009. By how much did it have to rise from the trough to regain the peak?
        Answer

        By 131%: 1,565.15/676.53 = 2.31. After a fall d, recovery needs a rise of d/(1 − d), not d. The recovery took 5.5 years from the peak, until 28 March 2013; a 57% rise from the trough was already reached in September 2009, 1.9 years after the peak.

        Volatility Regimes: the VIX

          Question for the room The mean VIX since 2000 is 19.8. From which lower threshold are at least half of the days below it? Check by moving the lower threshold.
          Answer

          From 18: the VIX is below 18 on 51% of the days and below 17 on 45%. The median, 17.8, is below the mean of 19.8: stress spikes stretch the right tail and lift the mean, but not the median. The VIX was below 20 on 62% of the days and at least 30 on 9%. The highest values are 82.7 on 16 March 2020, 80.9 on 20 November 2008 and 52.3 on 8 April 2025: calm periods are long, stress periods short and intense.

          Diversification Benefits Change over Time

            Question for the room The mean 252-day rolling correlation between the S&P 500 and TLT was −0.43 in 2010–2020 and +0.08 in 2022–2026. The ▶ button lengthens the window up to 756 days. What happens to the 2022–2026 mean?
            Answer

            It stays close to zero: +0.10 with the 63-day window, +0.08 with 252 and −0.02 with 756, while the 2010–2020 mean stays between −0.42 and −0.44. The change does not come from the window: since 2022, TLT no longer gains, on average, on the days when stocks lose. Computed directly on each subperiod, the correlation is −0.47 and +0.11. The S&P 500–Bitcoin correlation rose from 0.01 (2016–2019) to 0.38 (2022–2026).

            The Romanian Market

            The BET Index, 1997–2026: a Long Recovery

              Question for the room How long did the BET take to regain its peak of 24 July 2007?
              Answer

              13.6 years. The index fell by 82.5% in 1.6 years, until 25 February 2009, and only returned to the peak level on 16 March 2021; the S&P 500 regained its 2007 peak in 5.5 years. Over the whole of 1997–2026 the BET grew 32.8 times, a CAGR of 12.8%.

              Digital Assets and Tokenisation

              Stablecoins and the Peg

                Question for the room On what share of days did USDT stay within 0.5% of 1 USD in 2019–2026?
                Answer

                On 93% of the days, and within 1% on 98%. The peg does not hold every day, but the deviations stay of the order of a percent, far from the moves of Bitcoin. The extreme deviations are from March 2020: 1.054 USD on 12 March and 0.974 USD on 18 March.

                Passive Investing and Concentration

                Cap-Weighted versus Equal-Weighted Performance: SPY and RSP

                  Question for the room Since April 2003 SPY has grown 12.7 times and RSP 11.9 times. The ▶ button moves the start year up to 2025. For which start years does SPY end ahead of RSP?
                  Answer

                  For every start year in 2003–2025: the final SPY/RSP ratio lies between 1.07 (start in 2003 or 2025) and 1.40 (start in 2015). The final ratio changes with the start year because each window starts from a different point of the ratio. The conclusion depends on the end date, though: the ratio, equal to 1 in April 2003, had fallen to 0.76 in April 2015, and the SPY advantage comes from the 31% rise of the ratio since January 2023. A higher realised return does not show an expected premium.

                  Case Studies: Stock Market Competition and Treasury Bond Risk

                  Case Study: an Illustrative Scenario, 30% Turn Passive

                    Question for the room 30% of investors turn passive (α = 0.7). The ▶ button raises the strategic response χ up to 10. How does the pass-through rate 1/(1 + χα) change as χ rises?
                    Answer

                    It falls from 1.00 (χ = 0) to 0.32 (χ = 2.97, the paper's estimate) and to 0.13 (χ = 10): active investors offset a growing part of each move to passive management. The pass-through rate is not the active share α. The aggregate elasticity left, eq. (5), is 0.70 with χ = 0, 0.90 with χ = 2.97, so two-thirds of the fall is offset, and 0.96 with χ = 10.

                    Case Study: Treasury Bond–Stock Betas, 1967–2026

                      Question for the room Between 1979Q4 and 2001Q1 the quarterly beta of the 10-year nominal bond on stocks is 0.27. What is it in 2001Q2–2019Q4, and what does the sign change mean?
                      Answer

                      It is −0.34 (SE 0.06): after 2001 the nominal bond gains when stocks lose, so it hedges an equity portfolio. In 2020Q1–2026Q2 the nominal beta is 0.00 (SE 0.17) and the TIPS beta 0.19 (SE 0.13). On the daily returns of 2026 (up to 18 September) the nominal beta, 0.22, rises above the TIPS beta, 0.17; on the 120-day window of the chart the values on 18 September are 0.27 and 0.19.

                      AI for Scientific Discovery

                      Mini-Case: Estimate, Interval and MDE

                        Question for the room Over the full sample the mean SPY − RSP difference is +0.29% a year, with t = 0.27. Does this mean that there is no concentration premium?
                        Answer

                        No. The minimum detectable effect is 3.1% a year, so a smaller true premium would usually go undetected. The two-sided p-value, about 0.79, is not the probability that the premium exists or is absent. The sign changes between the halves: −2.28% (t = −1.75) in 2003–2014 and +2.85% (t = 1.63) in 2015–2026.
