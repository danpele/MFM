From Markowitz to the CAPM

The Efficient Frontier of US Sectors

    Question for the room SPY has a Sharpe ratio of 0.49 and the tangency portfolio of the sectors 0.66. Does this distance reject the CAPM?
    Answer

    No. With Mkt-RF as the market, the GRS test on the 9 sectors gives 0.53, with p = 0.85. The tangency portfolio is estimated in sample and holds large short positions: −0.37 in XLF and −0.21 in XLB.

    Estimating Beta

    The Beta of the Technology ETF (XLK) in the Data

      Question for the room The standard error of the XLK beta is 0.041 with the classical formula and 0.082 with Newey–West. Does the estimate beta = 1.277 change as well?
      Answer

      No. HAC errors change only the standard errors; the point estimates stay the OLS ones. The annual alpha is 0.33%, with t = 0.13, and the 95% bootstrap interval is [−5.5%, 4.9%], so alpha does not differ from zero.

      Choices That Change an Estimated Beta

        Question for the room A stock with a beta of 1 trades on only 75% of the days. What slope does the regression on the same-day market return give?
        Answer

        0.77, so beta is underestimated. The slope on the previous-day market return is 0.16, and the Dimson beta, the sum of the three slopes, is 0.93.

        Betas Are Not Constant

          Question for the room Between which values did the XLE beta move over 252-day windows?
          Answer

          Between −0.31 and 1.49; at the end of the sample it is −0.29, because oil moved against the market. The XLF beta moved between 0.60 and 2.17, with its maximum right after the 2008–2009 banking crisis.

          Testing the CAPM

          The Test Assets: 25 Size × B/M Portfolios

            Question for the room In each size row, which B/M column has the highest mean return?
            Answer

            The value column (BM5). The extreme corners are small growth, with 3.9% a year, and small value, with 13.3% a year. The CAPM alphas keep the same order: −6.3% a year for small growth and +5.7% for small value.

            The Security Market Line in the Data

              Question for the room The slope of the CAPM line is the market premium, 7.2% a year. What slope does the line fitted on the 25 portfolios have?
              Answer

              −4.4% a year: portfolios with a higher beta have lower mean returns. The betas cover only 0.86–1.42, 10 of the 25 alphas have |t| > 1.96, and GRS = 4.20, with p = 8 × 10−11.

              Multifactor Models

              Cumulative Returns of the Six Factors, 1963–2026

                Question for the room Which factor has the highest final value of one unit invested in July 1963?
                Answer

                MOM, with 48.0, followed by Mkt-RF with 43.2. HML ends at 6.9 and SMB at 2.9. Momentum lost heavily when the market rebounded in 2009, and the value factor stagnated from about 2007 to 2020.

                Fama–MacBeth Regressions

                Estimated Risk Premia

                  Question for the room In FF3, the Fama–MacBeth market premium is −6.5% a year, while the mean of the factor is 7.2%. Why are they so far apart?
                  Answer

                  The market betas differ little across the 25 portfolios (0.89–1.14 in FF3), so the market premium is weakly identified and the intercept absorbs the average level of returns. For HML, the estimated premium (4.0%) is close to the factor mean (3.6%).

                  Latent Factors: PCA and IPCA

                  PCA of the US Sector ETFs

                    Question for the room What share of the variance of the 11 sectors does the first principal component explain?
                    Answer

                    66.0%. All its loadings are positive (0.24–0.34), and the correlation of PC1 with SPY is 0.954. PC2 (9.7%) sets defensive sectors, such as utilities (+0.55), against growth sectors, such as technology (−0.40).

                    Factor Investing

                    Factor ETFs versus the Market

                      Question for the room Which factor ETFs have had a higher compound annual growth rate (CAGR) than SPY since July 2013?
                      Answer

                      Only IWF (16.3%) and MTUM (15.5%), against 14.0% for SPY. QUAL has 13.5%, VLUE 13.1%, IWD 10.7%, USMV 10.5% and IWM 9.3%. Thirteen years are a single historical path, too short to reject the factor premia.

                      AI for Scientific Discovery

                      Mini-Case: Was Momentum Arbitraged Away?

                        Question for the room After the 1993 publication, the momentum mean falls by 0.45 percentage points a month. Is the decline significant at 5%?
                        Answer

                        No: t = −1.45, and the 95% interval is [−1.05%, 0.16%]. The decline is 54% of the in-sample mean of 0.82% a month, close to the 58% in McLean and Pontiff (2016). For Mkt-RF (placebo), b₂ = +0.40%, with t = 1.12.
