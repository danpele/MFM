Prices and Returns

Five Markets: Prices and Daily Log Returns

    Question for the room How often does the EUR/RON rate move by more than 1% in a day?
    Answer

    On 1.4% of the days, against 25.5% of the days for the S&P 500 and 60.8% for Bitcoin. Under the Normal distribution with the EUR/RON daily volatility, 0.28%, a move of more than 1% would occur on only 0.03% of the days. The worst Bitcoin day, 12 March 2020, has a log return of −46.5%: the price is multiplied by e−0.465, so it falls by 37.2%.

    Distribution and Tails

    Fact 2, Heavy Tails: Histograms and Quantile–Quantile (QQ) Plots

      Question for the room The Student-t distribution fits the S&P 500 histogram well. Does it therefore describe the tails correctly?
      Answer

      Not necessarily. The histogram is dominated by the centre, where almost all the days lie. The tails are judged separately, on the QQ plot and with the Hill estimator: for the S&P 500, 0.67% of the days have |z| > 4, against 0.006% under the Normal distribution, and the estimated tail index is α̂ = 3.07.

      Hill Plots for Five Markets

        Question for the room Is the fourth moment of returns finite?
        Answer

        The estimates cast doubt on it. At k = 2.5% of n, α = 4 lies outside every 95% interval except the one for Bitcoin (α̂ = 4.22). The result holds with the block-bootstrap standard errors, which are 1.1–1.6 times the i.i.d. ones. The conclusion is conditional on the Pareto tail approximation and on the threshold used: move k/n and watch how α̂ changes.

        Fact 3: Gain/Loss Asymmetry in the Data

          Question for the room For the S&P 500, VaR 1% exceeds the 99% quantile of gains. Is the difference significant?
          Answer

          No. Δ = 3.15 − 2.97 = 0.18 pp, and the 95% block-bootstrap confidence interval, [−0.12, 0.49] pp, contains 0. The only significant difference is on EUR/RON, with the opposite sign: Δ = −0.17 pp, interval [−0.35, −0.08] pp.

          Dependence and Its Mechanisms

          Facts 1 and 6: Uncorrelated but Not Independent

            Question for the room How many of the first 100 autocorrelations of S&P 500 returns leave the 95% band?
            Answer

            20 leave the i.i.d. band and only 7 the robust band; at 5%, about 5 in 100 leave it by chance. The autocorrelations of |rt| and rt2 are positive at all 100 lags: the autocorrelations of returns are small, except at lag 1 (ρ̂₁ = −0.08), but returns are not independent.

            Fact 5: Intermittency in the Data

              Question for the room What happened to EUR/RON volatility after June 2013?
              Answer

              It fell sharply, under the managed float of the BNR. On this page, the mean of the 63-day rolling volatility drops from 5.8% before July 2013 to 2.0% after. In the lecture, the variance-break test dates the change on 27 June 2013, and the annualised volatility of the two subsamples is 6.6% before and 2.2% after.

              Fact 4: Aggregational Gaussianity

                Question for the room Do 60-day returns reach the Normal distribution?
                Answer

                Only on some markets. At h = 60, the excess kurtosis falls from 10.9 to 1.6 for the S&P 500 (nh = 154, z = 4.1) and from 12.0 to 0.1 for Bitcoin (z = 0.1). Normality is still rejected for the S&P 500, BET-TR (z = 3.0) and EUR/RON (z = 20).

                Fact 9: The Leverage Effect

                  Question for the room Does S&P 500 volatility rise more after falls than after rises?
                  Answer

                  Yes. L(1) = −0.12, with z = −5.4 using the robust standard error, and it is still −0.07 at k = 10. After the worst 5% of days, |rt+1| averages 1.40%; after the best 5%, only 1.04%. On EUR/RON the effect is reversed: L(1) = +0.10 (z = 2.5).

                  Fact 10: Volume and Volatility Move Together

                    Question for the room Are days with unusually high volume also days with large price moves?
                    Answer

                    Yes. The Spearman correlation between abnormal volume and |rt| is 0.25 for the S&P 500, interval [0.22, 0.28], and 0.39 for Bitcoin, interval [0.34, 0.44]. For Bitcoin, the volume reported by exchanges includes wash trading.

                    Persistence and the Limits of Inference

                    Fact 8: Slow Decay of the ACF of |rₜ|

                      Question for the room Does the slow decay of the ACF of |rt| prove long memory?
                      Answer

                      No. For the S&P 500 the ACF is 0.27 at lag 1, 0.22 at lag 20 and still 0.09 at lag 100, far above an exponential decay. Structural breaks in the level of volatility produce the same pattern, though. On the five markets, the semiparametric estimators give d̂ between 0.35 and 0.50 (S&P 500: 0.48 with local Whittle, 0.50 with the log-periodogram).

                      The Taylor Effect

                        Question for the room For which power δ is the autocorrelation of |rt|δ highest on the S&P 500?
                        Answer

                        Below 2 at the lags in the chart: δ = 1.75 at lag 1, 1.5 at lag 5 and 1.25 at lag 20; at lag 2, the peak is at δ = 2 itself. At lag 1 the peak is closer to 2 than to 1. As the lag grows, the peak moves down towards 1 and, at most lags beyond 30, below 1 (0.5–0.75), where the curves are almost flat. The position of the peak has no standard error, so the comparison with δ = 1 is descriptive.

                        Volatility from Open, High, Low and Close

                        Open, High, Low and Close (OHLC) Prices of One Day

                          Question for the room On a given day the close equals the open. Does the range of the day say anything about volatility?
                          Answer

                          Yes. The intraday return is zero, but the range ht − lt stays large if the price swung widely. For a Brownian motion, E[(ht − lt)2] = 4 ln 2 · σ2, and the Parkinson estimator has an efficiency of about 5 relative to close-to-close.

                          Volatility Estimators on the S&P 500

                            Question for the room Why do the range-based estimators give lower volatilities than close-to-close on the S&P 500?
                            Answer

                            Because they miss the overnight jumps. On 21-day windows, 2008–2026, the mean is 16.4% for close-to-close, 13.9% for Yang–Zhang, 13.0% for Parkinson, 12.4% for Garman–Klass and 12.3% for Rogers–Satchell. The range-based series are 3–4 times smoother, but this does not prove efficiency.

                            Performance and Drawdowns

                            Drawdowns Across Markets

                              Question for the room What gain is needed to recover the S&P 500 drawdown of 2007–2009?
                              Answer

                              +131%. The index fell from 1,565.15 (9 October 2007) to 676.53 (9 March 2009), i.e. by 56.8%, and after a fall x, regaining the peak requires a gain of 1/(1 − x) − 1. The drawdown on the log scale, −ln(1 − 0.568) = 0.84, is not the gain needed: e0.84 − 1 = 131%.

                              Risk and Return Across Markets

                                Question for the room Does the chart show that BET-TR was the best investment of the five?
                                Answer

                                No. The periods differ, and with rf = 0 the BET-TR Sharpe ratio (1.34, SE 0.29) is overstated: the index includes dividends and is in lei, with interest rates far above those in USD. With a common start in 2015, the Sharpe ratio is 1.38 for BET-TR, 1.05 for Bitcoin and 0.73 for the S&P 500, but the standard errors are about 0.3, so the ranking is not reliable.

                                Case Studies: Path-Dependent Volatility and the Overnight Return

                                Case Study (3/4): Observed VIX and VIX Fitted from S&P 500 Returns

                                  Question for the room How much of the VIX do past S&P 500 returns alone explain?
                                  Answer

                                  On the course data, R2 = 0.947 in training (2000–2018) and 0.863 in the test (2019 – May 2022), with RMSE 0.020 and 0.034. The last 5 days carry 20% of the weight of the volatility kernel, and lags beyond 250 days still 10%.

                                  The Market Return Around the Clock (2/4): SPY Overnight and Intraday

                                    Question for the room When was the SPY return earned in 1993–2026: overnight or intraday?
                                    Answer

                                    Overnight. The overnight mean is 7.7% a year (t = 4.6), the intraday mean 0.8% a year (t = 0.33), and 91% of the cumulative log price return was earned overnight. Overnight volatility, 10.6%, is nevertheless lower than intraday volatility, 15.2%. Dividends add 1.8% a year.
