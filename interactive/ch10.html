The Limit Order Book

A Simulated Order Book

    Question for the room How far from the mid-price do most limit orders sit in the simulated book?
    Answer

    Away from the mid. The average depth is 0.8 orders at 1 tick from the mid, 4.8 at 10 ticks and 5.0 at 25 ticks. Near the mid, market orders consume liquidity faster than limit orders replenish it; the spread averages 3.23 ticks.

    The Price Impact of a Market Order

      Question for the room A market buy order of 80 units is 80 times larger than one of a single unit. How many times further from the mid does it reach?
      Answer

      About 12 times: from 1.7 to 20.1 ticks. On log axes the slope is 0.53, close to 1/2, so the impact is concave. The average execution price rises with slope 0.44: the 80 units pay 11.9 ticks per unit on average.

      The Bid-Ask Spread

      The Bid-Ask Bounce (Simulated)

        Question for the room The efficient price is a random walk. Are the changes of the trade price uncorrelated?
        Answer

        No. Their first-order autocorrelation is −0.458, close to the theoretical value −c²/(σ² + 2c²) = −0.463. The Roll estimator gives ŝ = 0.0998, against the true spread 2c = 0.10. Beyond lag 1 the autocorrelations stay inside the 95% band.

        Spread Estimators at Two Frequencies: SPY

          Question for the room One tick is 0.21 bp of the SPY price. Which estimator comes closest to this value?
          Answer

          EDGE on 5-minute bars: 0.46 bp; the 95% interval for the mean of the estimator, from a 20-day block bootstrap, is [0.41, 0.51]. On daily bars all estimates are two orders of magnitude above one tick (Roll 64.3, Corwin–Schultz 26.5, Abdi–Ranaldo 55.1 and EDGE 40.1 bp), because the daily range is dominated by volatility.

          Spread Estimates Across 25 Assets

            Question for the room The median Corwin–Schultz estimate is 53 bp in the US and 54 bp on the BVB. Are BVB stocks as liquid as large US companies?
            Answer

            No. The median daily traded value is USD 10,505 million in the US and USD 0.34 million on the BVB, a ratio of about 31,000. Estimates from daily bars rank the assets by volatility (the Spearman correlation with annualised volatility is 0.94), and Abdi–Ranaldo is 0 for 8 of the 25 assets.

            Illiquidity and Intraday Patterns

            Amihud Illiquidity Across 25 Assets

              Question for the room How many times larger is the Amihud ratio of Banca Transilvania, the most liquid BVB stock, than that of SPY?
              Answer

              About 17,600 times: 28.0 bp against 0.0016 bp per USD 1 million traded. Bitcoin has 0.0037 bp, close to SPY, and Transelectrica 1,024.0 bp. The Amihud ratio separates the three markets by orders of magnitude, which the spread estimators from daily bars do not.

              Illiquidity over Time: Three Markets

                Question for the room Did BVB illiquidity fall after Romania became an emerging market in September 2020?
                Answer

                There is no visible break in September 2020. The BVB median declines gradually, from 435 bp per USD 1 million in 2017 to 169 bp in 2026, and remains about 7,000 times the US median (0.023 bp in 2026). The largest decline is in crypto: from 7.6 bp to 0.089 bp.

                SPY Within the Trading Day

                  Question for the room What share of the daily SPY volume trades in the last 30 minutes of the session?
                  Answer

                  18.6%, against 12.7% in the first 30 minutes and 0.9% for one 5-minute bar at midday. The mean absolute return is 10.6 bp in the first bar and 5.1 bp at midday, a ratio of 2.06.

                  Liquidity and Volatility: SPY and the VIX

                    Question for the room Does SPY illiquidity rise when the VIX rises?
                    Answer

                    Yes. The correlation is 0.58 on daily data and 0.69 on monthly means, and the elasticity of log illiquidity with respect to the log VIX is 0.95 (HAC standard error 0.12). In April 2025 illiquidity was 3.04 bp per USD 100 million, against a mean of 1.83.

                    Information and Prices

                    Kyle's Lambda and the Insider's Profit

                      Question for the room What happens to Kyle's lambda and to the insider's profit if the standard deviation of noise-trader orders doubles?
                      Answer

                      λ = σv/(2σu) halves, so market depth 1/λ doubles; the expected profit σvσu/2 doubles as well. The insider trades more aggressively (β = σu/σv), and the price still reveals half of the private information.

                      Price Impact and Optimal Execution

                      Optimal Trajectories and the Efficient Frontier

                        Question for the room What does it cost to reduce the risk of selling 1 million shares over 5 days?
                        Answer

                        With λ = 2·10⁻⁶, the standard deviation of the cost falls from USD 1.04 million (selling evenly) to USD 0.45 million, and the expected cost rises by USD 0.48 million, from 0.60 to 1.08 million. 57% of the position is sold on the first day. The frontier is convex: the first reductions of risk are cheap, the last ones expensive.

                        High-Frequency Trading and Market Stress

                        The Flash Crash of 6 May 2010

                          Question for the room How far did SPY fall on 6 May 2010, at the low of the day and at the close?
                          Answer

                          The low, USD 105.00, was 10.1% below the previous close (USD 116.82), but the close was only 3.3% lower. Volume was 3.0 times the median of the preceding days. Daily bars show the range, not the fall and the recovery within the day.

                          Case Studies: the HFT Arms Race and Intraday Momentum

                          Case Study: the Implied Daily Tax on SPY

                            Question for the room On how many days does the implied SPY tax exceed the authors' most pessimistic scenario, 0.74 bp?
                            Answer

                            On 2 of the 1,244 days. The maximum, 1.04 bp, is on 7 April 2025, with a realised volatility of 106.7%. The median tax is 0.40 bp, close to the 0.419 bp measured in London, and on 30% of the days the tax exceeds 0.4213 bp.

                            Case Study: SPY Intraday Momentum, 2020–2026

                              Question for the room Does the last half hour of SPY continue the move of the rest of the day, as in the paper's futures?
                              Answer

                              We find no significant positive momentum. Over the 1,460 days of Oct 2020 – Sep 2026 the slope is 100β = −0.32 (t = −0.33), with the 95% interval [−2.22, 1.58]: the paper's slope, 4.18, lies outside it. The only period whose interval excludes 0 is 2020-21, with slope −4.81, of the opposite sign (p = 0.019; after the Holm correction for six subperiods, p = 0.11). For the last 10 minutes the slope is −1.48 (p = 0.043): a negative coefficient, not significant after the Holm correction for six intervals (p = 0.26).

                              Mini-Case: Cost or Volatility?

                                Question for the room Do the Corwin–Schultz ranks from daily bars follow the cost of trading or volatility?
                                Answer

                                Volatility. The Spearman correlation with annualised volatility is 0.94, and with the Amihud ratio −0.12 (p = 0.58).
