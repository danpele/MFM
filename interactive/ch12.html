Black–Scholes and the Greeks

The Greeks: One Identity, All Sensitivities

    Question for the room Which expiry has the largest gamma at the strike, and which has the largest vega?
    Answer

    The one-month call has the largest gamma and theta at K = 100, and the one-year call has the largest vega. For one expiry, vega = ΓS²στ: at S = 100 and σ = 20%, the three-month call has a vega of 0.197 per volatility point.

    Delta Hedging in Practice

    Discrete Delta Hedging: Algorithm and Error

      Question for the room With daily rebalancing, the standard deviation of the error is 9.5% of the premium. How much more often must the hedge be adjusted to halve it?
      Answer

      About four times as often, because the standard deviation falls like 1/√N (the estimated log-log slope is −0.48). With N = 252, four times a day, it is 4.8% of the premium. Transaction costs, however, grow in proportion to N.

      Selling Volatility at 20%: Three Outcomes

        Question for the room A three-month call is sold at 20% implied volatility and hedged daily. What is the mean P&L of the seller if realised volatility is 25%?
        Answer

        −0.99 per option, positive on only 2.2% of the paths. The mean is close to vega × 5 volatility points = 0.197 × 5. With realised volatility of 15%, the mean is +0.98, positive on 99.8% of the paths.

        Implied Volatility and the Smile

        Three Sources of a Smile

          Question for the room Which of the three sources gives the steepest left wing at K/S = 0.8?
          Answer

          Merton jumps: 31.0%, against 16.3% ATM, close to the historical distribution (30.6%, against 13.9% ATM). Heston with ρ = −0.7 gives 28.6%, and its right wing is the lowest (15.0% at K/S = 1.2), because a negative correlation tilts the smile to the left.

          Bitcoin Options and the Volatility Surface

          The Bitcoin Smile

            Question for the room Is the Bitcoin smile a skew, as for equity indices?
            Answer

            No. Both wings rise, because Bitcoin can jump up as well as down. Puts are still dearer than calls at the same |k|: the skew is negative at all 9 expiries. The root mean squared error of the SVI fits is at most 0.70 volatility points.

            What the Market Prices for Bitcoin

              Question for the room According to option prices, how likely is a fall of Bitcoin by more than 30% within 38 days?
              Answer

              1.21%, with a bootstrap interval of 1.08–1.24%, about 8 times the 0.14% of a log-normal distribution with the ATM volatility of 36.4%. 0.13 of the 1.21 points lie below the lowest quoted strike (0.47F), where the density is an SVI extrapolation.

              The VIX and the Variance Risk Premium

              The VIX, 1990–2026

                Question for the room How often was the VIX above 30 between 1990 and 2026?
                Answer

                On 7.9% of days. The mean is 19.4, and the maximum, 82.7, was reached on 16 March 2020. Spikes are sudden and decay slowly, as in GARCH models.

                Implied vs Realised Volatility, S&P 500

                  Question for the room On what share of days is the VIX above the volatility realised over the next 21 trading days?
                  Answer

                  On 86% of days. The mean premium is 4.09 volatility points (Newey–West standard error 0.27), or 113 %² in variance, with t = 5.5. The exceptions are crashes: the worst window, starting on 20 February 2020, has −72.0 points.

                  Is the VIX a Good Forecast?

                    Question for the room Taken separately, neither α̂ = −51.9 nor β̂ = 0.86 differs significantly from (0, 1). Is the VIX an unbiased forecast?
                    Answer

                    No. The two estimates have a correlation of −0.90, and the joint Wald test gives χ²₂ = 41.7, with p = 8.9·10⁻¹⁰. The VIX explains R² = 0.37 of the subsequent realised variance, past realised variance only 0.29.

                    Options with Zero Days to Expiry

                    Gamma and Time Decay Near Expiry

                      Question for the room How much larger is the ATM gamma one trading day before expiry than one month before?
                      Answer

                      4.6 times (0.396 against 0.086), i.e. √21, because the ATM gamma is proportional to 1/√τ. A 1% move in the last day changes delta by about 0.4. Time decay is 0.167 in the last calendar day, against 0.030 with 30 days left.

                      Case Study: Are Option-Implied Bounds Valid and Tight?

                      Case Study: the Bound and the Realised Premium

                        Question for the room The realised 12-month excess return is below the Martin bound on 33% of dates. Does this contradict the validity of the bound?
                        Answer

                        No. The bound restricts the conditional mean E_t[R^e], not each realisation. The paper does not reject validity (D₁ = 0 at every horizon) but rejects tightness: the mean slackness is 4.8–5.2 points a year. The correlation between the bound and the realised return is only 0.15.
